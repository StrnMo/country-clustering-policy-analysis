FINAL CLUSTER SELECTION: k=5
============================================================


The five-cluster solution provides a useful level of granularity for 
distinguishing multidimensional socioeconomic profiles while remaining 
manageable for interpretation and policy communication.

1. CLUSTER BALANCE:
   • k=5: Cluster sizes range from 10 to 57 countries
   • k=4: The largest cluster contains 77 countries, potentially combining
     relatively heterogeneous socioeconomic profiles
   • k=6: The smallest clusters contain only 6 countries, making some
     profiles less robust and harder to interpret
   • k=7: One cluster contains only 4 countries, creating an overly
     granular solution

2. MULTIDIMENSIONAL INTERPRETABILITY:
   • k=5 provides five distinguishable socioeconomic profiles
   • k=4 may oversimplify some differences between country profiles
   • k=6 and k=7 introduce smaller and more specific groups
   • Five profiles provide a practical balance between detail and clarity

3. PRACTICAL APPLICABILITY:
   • Five clusters are manageable for policy analysis and communication
   • Each k=5 cluster contains at least 10 countries
   • Budget allocation and cluster-specific recommendations can be
     communicated more clearly using five groups
   • The resulting profiles can be interpreted as descriptive
     socioeconomic groupings rather than rigid development categories

4. TRADE-OFF BETWEEN VALIDATION METRICS AND INTERPRETABILITY:
   • No single value of k dominates across all validation metrics
   • k=4 achieves the highest Silhouette and Calinski-Harabasz scores
   • k=6 achieves the lowest Davies-Bouldin score
   • k=5 provides a balanced solution with reasonable cluster sizes,
     strong interpretability, and practical policy applicability
   • Therefore, k=5 was selected as a pragmatic compromise between
     statistical validation, cluster size, interpretability, and
     policy usefulness

5. INTERPRETATION OF THE FINAL CLUSTERS:
   • The resulting clusters are descriptive socioeconomic profiles
   • Labels such as Low-Development Profile, Developing-Economy Profile,
     High-Income Trade-Oriented Profile, Emerging-Economy Profile, and
     Advanced-Economy Profile are interpretations of the observed
     multidimensional patterns
   • These labels are not intended to reproduce or replace official
     World Bank, IMF, or UN classifications
